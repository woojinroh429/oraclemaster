# P7 and P22 replicated: the two big losses were the instances' own variance, not the change

The 37-instance paired validation of the aim portfolio (results/audit/mix40.md) came out 31 better
against 6 worse, and the only two losses above 10% were P7 (+38%) and P22 (+30%), both 150 blocks.
Three replicates per arm, same 180 s, same build, `data/stage2`.

    P7        ctl (all 0.90)      mix (portfolio)     mix vs ctl
    original                                            +38%
    r1           1,075,322              953,242        -11.35%
    r2             831,362            1,012,381        +21.78%

    P22       ctl                 mix                 mix vs ctl
    original                                            +30%
    r1               4,776                4,929         +3.20%
    r2               4,776                6,061        +26.90%

## What the control arm alone does

    P7   ctl:  1,075,322 -> 831,362      29.3% spread, same build, same budget
    P7   mix:    953,242 -> 1,012,381     6.2% spread

The gap between the arms on P7 (11% one way, 22% the other) is smaller than the gap the control
arm produces against itself. Every difference observed on this instance, the original +38%
included, is inside its own run-to-run variance. It is not evidence that the portfolio loses here,
and the r1 result is not evidence that it wins either -- I reported r1 as a sign flip and that was
reading noise as signal.

P22 is the opposite instance, and it is not nothing.

    P22  ctl:  4,776 -> 4,776       0% spread.  The control converges to the same answer twice.
    P22  mix:  4,929 -> 6,061      23% spread.

The control arm on P22 is deterministic and the portfolio arm is not. The portfolio loses twice
out of two, and it introduces variance into an instance that had none. That is the reverse of what
I predicted from the P7 pair -- I wrote that a portfolio widens the worker distribution and makes
the minimum steadier, and on this instance it makes it shakier.

The mechanism is the one aim.md set out and I then dropped when the 40-instance small group came
back 14-4. At 150 blocks the beam finishes comfortably, so the salvage the low aim exists to feed
never fires and the two low-aim workers are simply narrower for no return. The control takes a
minimum over four good workers and lands on 4,776 every time; the portfolio takes a minimum over
effectively two, and a minimum over two draws is both worse and more variable than over four.

So the small group contains at least one instance where the axis is genuinely and repeatably
harmful, with a mechanism that explains it. What is not established is how many: 14 of 18 small
instances improved, and one confirmed case does not tell us whether the other three losses share
this shape or are P7-style noise.

## What this changes

The adoption stands: 31-6 over 37 pairs, p < 0.0001, large group 17-2, and P22 is one instance.

What it changes is which question is open. It is no longer "are the two big losses real" -- one is
not and one is -- but "how many small instances behave like P22, and can they be told apart
without measuring instance size". The identifying signature is available in one run per arm:
OGC_WSTAT prints the four workers' objectives, and a P22-shaped instance should show the two
low-aim workers landing well above the two high-aim ones with nothing to show for the width they
gave up. harness/wstat.sh runs that over eight instances.

A gate on block count is still the wrong answer, and it would not even fit: P7 and P22 are both
150 blocks and only one of them is hurt.

What it adds is a measurement rule. Five single draws have now been replicated this session
(prob_20 -17.37% -> -0.9%, prob_15 +6.63% -> +3.0%, prob_34 -7.67% -> noise, P7 +38% -> both signs,
P22 +30% -> +3.2%) and all five shrank. A single paired draw on this problem is worth roughly its
sign, and only when the instance is large enough to be stable; a 150-block instance needs
replicates before any number from it is quoted.

## Final: twelve runs

```
P7          ctl            mix          paired
r1     1,075,322      953,242      -11.35%
r2       831,362    1,012,381      +21.77%
r3       923,531      953,242       +3.22%
mean     943,405      972,955      +4.55%   median +3.22%
range      29.3%         6.2%

P22         ctl            mix          paired
r1         4,776        4,929       +3.20%
r2         4,776        6,061      +26.91%
r3         4,929        4,929       +0.00%
mean       4,827        5,306     +10.03%   median +3.20%
range       3.2%        23.0%
```

The two single draws that prompted this, +38% and +30%, come out at +4.55% and +10.03% on three
replicates, and at +3.22% and +3.20% by median. The direction is consistent -- the portfolio never
wins P22 in three tries and loses P7 on two of three -- but the size is a third to a tenth of what
the single draw said. Against a set-wide median of -3.44% and -4.33% on the large group, two small
instances at roughly +3% is a price the axis pays for, not a reason to gate it.

## Two claims of mine that the third replicate broke

I called P7 a sign flip after r1 (-11.35%). Over three it is +4.55% mean: the portfolio is still
behind on that instance, and one replicate was not enough to say otherwise.

I called P22's control arm deterministic after two identical draws of 4,776. The third returned
4,929 -- the same value the portfolio arm returns -- so the contrast I drew between a stable
control and an unstable treatment does not hold. What survives is narrower: on P22 the portfolio
arm has the wider range (23.0% against 3.2%) and never wins.
